# SCAM Watch Methodology

SCAM Watch publishes evidence-based consumer fraud warnings. The objective is accuracy, traceability, proportional classification, and prompt correction when better evidence becomes available.

## Classification framework

### Confirmed Fraud
Use only when strong evidence establishes fraudulent conduct. Examples can include documentary confirmation of non-delivery or deception, platform or payment-provider findings, official agency or court outcomes, admissions, or equivalent independently verifiable evidence.

### Likely Scam
Use when multiple strong indicators support a scam conclusion but the evidence does not satisfy the Confirmed Fraud threshold. Examples include demonstrably copied sale material combined with suspicious payment demands, contradictory seller histories, account removal, or other corroborating facts.

### High Risk
Use when significant warning signs justify heightened caution but the available record is insufficient to conclude that fraud occurred.

### Unverified
Use when the available information is too limited to support a substantive assessment.

### Cleared / Resolved
Use when later reliable evidence establishes that a report was mistaken, the transaction was legitimate, or the underlying issue was resolved.

## Identity standard

A seller name, profile name, photograph, email address, telephone number, payment handle, or other identifier displayed by an account is not automatically proof of the real person controlling that account. Reports should use formulations such as **“seller identity presented as…”** unless identity has been independently verified.

## Evidence hierarchy

Higher-weight evidence includes original listing material, archived pages, transaction records, direct platform notices, payment-provider records, documented communications, independently dated source material demonstrating copied content, official records, and corroboration from independent sources.

Lower-weight evidence includes anonymous accusations, unsupported social posts, inference from price alone, or identity assumptions based solely on a displayed name or photograph.

## Publication principles

1. State what the evidence establishes and identify material uncertainty.
2. Preserve source URLs and dates where possible.
3. Distinguish fact, inference, and allegation.
4. Do not publish unnecessary private financial or personal data.
5. Do not infer that two accounts are controlled by the same person without evidence.
6. Record the date reviewed and the date materially updated.
7. Correct classifications when new evidence warrants a change.
8. Do not accept payment in exchange for suppressing accurate public-interest findings.

## Suggested incident fields

Each published incident should include a stable incident ID, title, classification, confidence/evidence notes, date reviewed, last-updated date, platform, product or service category, seller identity as presented, listing location if relevant, asking price if relevant, source/evidence URLs, payment method requested, red flags, factual narrative, privacy/redaction notes, and correction status.
