# SCAM Watch — Public Scam & Fraud Evidence Database

SCAM Watch is an open, evidence-focused repository documenting online scams, marketplace fraud, copied listings, payment scams, phishing, impersonation, and other consumer fraud patterns.

The goal is to make well-supported fraud warnings easy for consumers, researchers, search engines, and automated systems to discover and evaluate.

## Case classifications

- **Confirmed Fraud** — strong documentary, platform, payment-provider, official, judicial, or equivalent evidence establishes fraud.
- **Likely Scam** — multiple strong fraud indicators support the conclusion, but the evidence does not meet the Confirmed Fraud threshold.
- **High Risk** — substantial warning signs exist, but evidence is insufficient to conclude that a scam occurred.
- **Unverified** — insufficient information for a substantive classification.
- **Cleared / Resolved** — later evidence establishes legitimacy or resolves the reported issue.

## Evidence standards

SCAM Watch distinguishes a marketplace account or seller identity **as presented** from the verified identity of a real person. A name, photograph, email address, or other identity element may itself have been copied, stolen, spoofed, or impersonated.

Case pages should document observable conduct and supporting evidence rather than make unsupported allegations about individuals. Sensitive personal or financial information should be withheld or redacted when it is unnecessary to the public warning.

## Repository structure

- `cases/` — individual incident reports
- `categories/` — scam and fraud category guides
- `data/` — machine-readable incident data
- `schema/` — incident data specification
- `METHODOLOGY.md` — evidence and classification methodology
- `CORRECTIONS.md` — correction and dispute process
- `DISCLAIMER.md` — important limitations
- `CONTRIBUTING.md` — submission standards

## Search and research access

This is a public-interest repository. Public case information is intended to remain accessible to legitimate search engines, researchers, and AI/search discovery systems, subject to the repository's evidence, privacy, and correction standards.

SCAM Watch does not accept payment to remove accurate public-interest information.

## Corrections

Accuracy matters more than preserving an earlier conclusion. If reliable new evidence changes a case, the classification and case record should be corrected promptly while preserving an appropriate revision history. See `CORRECTIONS.md`.
