# Corrections, Disputes, and Updates

SCAM Watch is designed to be correctable. Publication is not a claim of infallibility.

If a person, business, platform, researcher, or consumer can provide reliable information showing that a published fact or classification is inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, or misleading, the evidence should be reviewed and the record corrected when warranted.

## Review principles

- Evidence is evaluated on substance, not on whether a request favors or disfavors a published subject.
- Material factual errors should be corrected promptly once verified.
- A classification may be upgraded, downgraded, or changed to **Cleared / Resolved** as evidence develops.
- A displayed account identity is not treated as verified personal identity unless independently established.
- Unnecessary personal or financial information may be redacted even when the underlying case remains published.
- Accurate public-interest information is not removed in exchange for payment.

## What a correction request should identify

A useful request identifies the incident ID or page, the specific statement disputed, the proposed correction, and reliable supporting evidence. Sensitive supporting material need not be published merely because it is used to verify a correction.

## Revision history

When practical, substantive changes should be reflected in repository history so researchers can understand how a case assessment evolved.
